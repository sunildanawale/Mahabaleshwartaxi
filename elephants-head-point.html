Elephant's Head Point

The Marvelous Natural Needle Hole Rock Formation

Back to Previous Page

Nature's Incredible Sculpting

Located right next to Kate's Point, Elephant's Head Point is one of the most fascinating geological wonders in Mahabaleshwar. This natural rock formation dramatically extends outward from the mountain face and remarkably resembles the head and trunk of an elephant, giving the point its name.

Also commonly known as Needle Hole Point, this location gets its alternate name from a natural hole that has formed between the elephant's trunk and its legs. When viewed from a specific angle, the gap looks exactly like the eye of a needle. This unique combination of formations makes it a highly sought-after spot for tourists and geology enthusiasts alike.

Beyond the rock formation itself, the point offers spectacular, vertiginous views of the Sahyadri mountain range. The extreme overhang provides a thrilling perspective of the deep valley below. The combination of the cool mountain breeze, the sweeping landscapes, and this peculiar rocky outcrop creates a memorable experience.

Being adjacent to Kate's Point, visiting Elephant's Head Point is seamless and forms a core part of the standard sightseeing itinerary.

Experiencing the Point

The Needle Hole

The natural gap in the rock is a marvel of wind and water erosion over thousands of years. Finding the exact angle to see the 'needle hole' clearly is a fun activity for visitors.

Thrilling Heights

The point extends far over the valley, offering a sheer drop that is both exhilarating and slightly terrifying. It provides a true sense of the altitude of Mahabaleshwar.

Monsoon Beauty

During the rainy season, the rock formation is surrounded by dense fog and vibrant green moss, turning the area into a mystical landscape straight out of a fantasy novel.

Tips for Visitors

Elephant's Head Point is accessed via the same parking area as Kate's Point. You will need to walk a short distance along a dirt path from Kate's Point to get the best view of the elephant formation. The path can get slightly muddy during the monsoons, so wearing shoes with good grip is advisable.

The viewpoint is heavily barricaded for safety, as the drops are extreme. Always respect the boundaries and do not attempt to cross the railings for a better photo-the designated viewing areas provide the best and safest angles.

This area is known for strong winds, especially in the late afternoons and evenings. Hold on tightly to hats, sunglasses, and lightweight items. Our local drivers usually guide guests on the best walking route to ensure you see both the Elephant's Head and the Needle Hole without confusion.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Kate's Point and Elephant's Head Point the same?

No, they are distinct viewpoints, but they are located right next to each other. You park at one location and can easily walk between Kate's Point, Echo Point, and Elephant's Head Point.

Can you actually see the shape of an elephant?

Yes, the resemblance is quite striking. The large rock projection clearly mimics the head of an elephant, complete with a trunk extending downwards.

Is there an entry fee to see the Needle Hole?

No, visiting Elephant's Head Point and seeing the Needle Hole rock formation is completely free of charge.

What is the best time of day to visit?

Morning to early afternoon is ideal. The lighting is good for photography, and you avoid the heavy crowds that sometimes gather later in the day.
