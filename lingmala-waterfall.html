Nature's Spectacular Cascade

The Glory of the Monsoons

Lingmala Waterfall is one of the most breathtaking natural attractions in Mahabaleshwar. Situated around 6 km from the town center along the Mahabaleshwar-Pune road, the Venna River cascades down from an astounding height of approximately 500 feet into the deep Venna Valley.

The site features two distinct waterfalls: the lower mini fall, which is calm and ideal for relaxing wading and swimming, and the thunderous main waterfall, viewed from an elevated vantage deck that provides panoramic valley vistas and misty rainbows on sunny days.

Lingmala is included in our popular Waterfall & Sunset Darshan (Tour 2), covered along with Wilson Point, Venna Lake, and Bombay Point at the official rate of ₹1,200.

Trek Path & Safety Advisory

Reaching the main waterfall requires a 1.5 km (approx. 30 minutes) nature walk with stone steps. During peak monsoons, paths can be slippery and misty.

Wear shoes with excellent grip (avoid smooth sandals or flip-flops)
Do not cross safety barricades at the elevated viewing deck
Carry an umbrella or light raincoat during rainy months

Highlights of Lingmala Waterfall

The 500-Ft Main Cascade

The thunderous plunge of the Venna River falling 500 feet into the canyon creates an awe-inspiring mist with frequent morning rainbows during monsoon and post-monsoon months.

Mini Bathing Pool

A calm, shallow natural stream pool located a short 10-minute walk from the forest entrance, safe for families and children to dip their feet in crystal-clear water.

Forest Nature Trail

The 1.5 km stone walkway winds through dense evergreen Sahyadri canopy, silver oak trees, and seasonal wildflowers teeming with butterflies and birdlife.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an entry fee for Lingmala Waterfall?

Yes, the forest department charges a nominal entry fee (approx ₹20-₹50 per person) and a parking fee for vehicles. This is not included in the taxi fare and must be paid at the counter.

Is there water in Lingmala Waterfall during summer?

During peak summer (April-May), the waterfall is often completely dry or reduced to a tiny trickle. The best time to visit is from July to October.

Can we swim in the waterfall?

You cannot swim or go near the main 600-ft waterfall. However, at the smaller lower waterfall, visitors are sometimes allowed to wade in the water, depending on the force of the current and official permissions on that day.

How much time is needed to visit?

Keep aside at least 1.5 to 2 hours for a visit. This accounts for the walk to the falls, time spent taking photos, and the walk back to the parking lot.
